# Chapter 6. Discussion

## 6.1 What the results show, taken together

**For most papers, no reproduction could be attempted.** In our main sample, the most common outcome was that no official code could be found from the paper and public sources. For five papers, a repository was found only on an author's GitHub account, with no link from the paper. Because our search for code was not exhaustive and authors were not contacted at this stage, this describes what a reader can find, not what exists.

**In several cases, verdicts depended on procedures the papers do not specify.** Two verdicts change under alternative readings of such procedures: the reported training epoch and the definition of the market average for AlphaMix+, and the code version and the evaluation window for MARS, whose authors' later revision of the code yields a reproduced verdict. In the exploratory reproduction of Markowitz Meets Bellman, the released script uses five stocks, whereas the paper describes thirty. We report both readings where a choice is open; we could not determine which reading the authors used, and we do not attribute the failed verdicts to these choices.

**Deviations in both directions.** The Relation-Aware Transformer reached a final portfolio value far above the reported one while its claimed ranking against baselines held. A result far above the reported value is not evidence of a stronger method, and we could not isolate its cause. Conversely, the ensemble strategy met the value criterion but not the comparative one, and in an exploratory experiment its Sharpe ratio showed weak evidence of sensitivity to a hard-coded turbulence threshold; no variant approached the reported value.

**For FinRL, the reproduced advantage over the index did not extend to an advantage over equal weight.** The comparative claim against the Dow Jones Industrial Average held for all four algorithms, yet under the unified protocol none retained an advantage over a daily-rebalanced equal-weight portfolio of the same assets. The protocol changed several things at once — costs, a cash start, additional windows and the comparator — so this does not isolate the effect of the comparator. In an exploratory analysis of the paper's test year, a deterministic PPO policy whose weights stayed within 0.001 of 1/N earned, at zero cost, a return above the index and within the tolerance band of the reported PPO value. The reproduced advantage over the index is therefore consistent with an allocation close to equal weight having outperformed the index in that period; the paper's comparison did not include equal weight. An indeterminate classification under the protocol is not evidence that no advantage exists. These statements concern one paper, one asset list and the windows tested.

**Configured costs were not always charged.** At the versions we examined, two of three official environments did not charge the transaction cost they were configured with (for AlphaMix+, established by reading the code), so results from these environments are zero-cost results whatever rate is configured. The third charged the cost with a formula that differs from the one in the paper, with a negligible effect at the paper's fee. A configuration value or a sentence in a paper was therefore not sufficient to establish that a cost was applied; a check with injected actions settled the question in each runnable case.

**Several trained policies were degenerate in ways that summary metrics did not reveal (exploratory).** The FinRL DDPG and TD3 policies became fixed-weight portfolios; the PPO policy was indistinguishable from its untrained initialization; and the stored runs behind the reported curve of Markowitz Meets Bellman were first-day buy-and-hold positions, whose composition, within the candidates chosen by the policy, was set by the environment's order filling and cash constraint. Cumulative return and Sharpe ratio alone did not reveal these degeneracies; PPO and TD3 met the reproduction criterion. They became visible through simple diagnostics: the share of actions at the boundary of the action space, day-to-day changes in target weights, comparison with untrained policies initialized with the same seed, and a test for unchanged holdings. The first-day buy-and-hold pattern was not found in two other share-based environments, and the other diagnostics were not applied there. We do not claim that degeneracy is common; we note only that, where it occurred, it was not visible in the reported summary metrics.

## 6.2 Relation to prior work

Near-equal-weight behaviour has been reported before for EIIE (Li, 2024) and for RL factor portfolios (André and Coqueret, 2020 [check: add to verified list]), and boundary-seeking actions are known in continuous control (Seyde et al., 2021). To our knowledge, our results add that such behaviour occurs in policies obtained from the official code of published methods, that in the FinRL portfolio environment the softmax over actions in [0, 1] bounds every weight to a narrow range around 1/N, and that the same methods' reproduced claims are consistent with this behaviour [check: whether Li (2024) used the official EIIE code]. That deep RL allocations did not outperform 1/N agrees with Kruthof and Müller (2025); our unit of analysis is the published method and its released code. The low availability of code agrees with a survey of RL trading studies (Felizardo et al., 2022) and a preprint audit of stock-prediction studies (Srikantha et al., 2026), and the dependence of verdicts on unspecified procedures echoes the role of seeds and evaluation choices in deep RL (Henderson et al., 2018).

## 6.3 Information and checks that would have allowed direct verdicts

We do not propose a reporting standard. The following list records, for each case in our results where a verdict or check could be made only conditionally or only by our own diagnostics, the information or check that would have allowed it directly.

1. **A link from the paper to the code, with the version used.** For five papers, the code was found only on an author's GitHub account, with no link from the paper; for MARS, the verdict depends on the code version.
2. **The number of runs and the rule by which the reported run or epoch was selected.** The AlphaMix+ verdict depends on it; DeepAries states it, which allowed the rule to be applied as written.
3. **The asset list and data period used by the released scripts.** The released script of Markowitz Meets Bellman uses five stocks; FinRL lost two stocks for lack of data at the start of training.
4. **An equal-weight baseline under the same costs.** For FinRL, it would have shown whether the advantage over the index extended to equal weight.
5. **Evidence that the configured cost is charged**, for example the cost incurred along an injected alternating path. Two of three environments did not charge it.
6. **A comparison with an untrained policy initialized with the same seed**, and the share of actions at the boundary of the action space. These identified the PPO policy as unchanged by training and the DDPG and TD3 policies as fixed portfolios.

These items do not by themselves make a method better or worse; they determine whether a reader can tell.

# Chapter 7. Conclusion

We asked whether published reinforcement-learning methods for portfolio allocation can be reproduced from the authors' code, and whether their advantage survives a common evaluation with transaction costs and an equal-weight baseline.

For most papers in our samples, no reproduction from the authors' code could be attempted, mainly because no official code could be found from the paper and public sources. Of six papers that received a verdict under criteria fixed in advance, none was fully reproduced with the code as of publication; three were partially reproduced and three were not. Within FinRL, two of four algorithms were reproduced individually, and MARS was reproduced with its authors' later revision of the code; the AlphaMix+ verdict changes with choices the paper leaves unspecified. Of three official environments checked, two did not charge the transaction cost they were configured with, and the third used a different formula with a negligible effect at the paper's fee.

For the one method eligible for re-evaluation under the pre-specified rule, the four FinRL agents retained no advantage over equal weight under a common protocol. Two lost it — exploratory diagnostics attribute most of the loss to turnover from the tutorial's stochastic prediction, and with deterministic prediction A2C is indeterminate — and two were indeterminate, with growth rates within a narrow margin of equal weight; an indeterminate classification is not evidence that no advantage exists. Exploratory diagnostics also showed that several trained policies were degenerate — fixed portfolios, an effectively untrained policy, and first-day buy-and-hold positions — in ways that their summary metrics did not reveal. Among the exploratory reproductions outside the fixed order, one (FRONTIER) met both criteria, against values read from a figure.

These results do not show that any paper's numbers are wrong or that RL cannot outperform equal weight. They show that, for the one method evaluated under the common protocol, an advantage reproduced against the index did not extend to an advantage over equal weight, and that for the other methods the code as of publication reproduced the reported values at most in part. The samples are not random, several methods share a code lineage, and all exploratory findings are specific to the code and settings examined.

Further work could extend the reproduction to the papers that passed the execution stages but were not given a verdict, add a second independent rater for the code-availability stage, obtain the authors' account of the unspecified procedures [check: align with Section 6.7 on author contact], and apply the cost and degeneracy checks to a larger and systematically drawn sample.
