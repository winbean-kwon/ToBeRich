## Abstract

Papers on deep reinforcement learning (RL) for portfolio allocation report outperformance, but whether the authors' code yields these results, and whether they survive transaction costs and an equal-weight (1/N) baseline, has rarely been tested systematically on the official code. We followed 69 papers through fixed stages from eligibility to a reproduction verdict; ran, under criteria fixed in advance, the official code of four pre-selected methods and of two papers that reached the last stage; checked whether three official environments charge their configured costs; and re-evaluated the one eligible method (FinRL) against 1/N under a common protocol. In the main sample, 31 of 52 papers exited at the code-availability stage. None of the six papers judged was fully reproduced with the code as of publication: three were partially reproduced (in FinRL, two of four algorithms individually) and three were not; one verdict changed with unspecified choices, and a later author revision of another met both criteria under one of two plausible evaluation windows. Two of three environments did not charge the configured cost. No FinRL agent retained an advantage over equal weight: two lost it (for A2C, only under the tutorial's stochastic prediction; exploratory) and two were indeterminate. Exploratory diagnostics showed fixed-weight and effectively untrained FinRL policies and, for a further paper, first-day buy-and-hold runs, none visible in summary metrics. These results do not show that reported numbers are wrong. They show that the code as of publication reproduced the reported values at most in part, and that, for the one method re-evaluated, an advantage over the index did not extend to equal weight. The samples are not random.
