## Abstract

This thesis examines whether reinforcement-learning (RL) portfolio methods that appear to outperform simple allocation still do so under careful evaluation. It first reports the author's development of an RL allocation system. A hierarchical policy over 44 cryptocurrency pairs earned a test Sharpe ratio of 2.60 against 0.26 for equal weight, but thirteen of sixteen training seeds had a negative Sharpe ratio in validation, most of its test result was reproduced by equal weight held along its exposure path, and about two thirds of its test log growth came from the evaluation's initial holding, an effect that overlaps with that exposure path; a pre-registered study showed that this start-state effect grows with a policy's inertia and follows the market's first days, adding variance rather than a consistent bias. In a held-out period evaluated once under criteria fixed in advance, its advantage over the same exposure was not maintained. The main study then asks the same question of published methods. We followed 69 papers through fixed stages from eligibility to a reproduction verdict; ran, under criteria fixed in advance, the official code of four pre-selected methods and of two papers that reached the last stage; checked whether three official environments charge their configured costs; and re-evaluated the one eligible method (FinRL) against equal weight (1/N) under a common protocol. In the main sample, 31 of 52 papers exited at the code-availability stage. None of the six papers judged was fully reproduced with the code as of publication: three were partially reproduced and three were not. Two of three environments did not charge the configured cost, and in four of thirteen implementations checked the training signal did not respond to it. No FinRL agent retained an advantage over equal weight: two lost it and two were indeterminate, and exploratory diagnostics showed fixed-weight and effectively untrained policies whose summary metrics did not reveal this. Where the source of an apparent advantage could be traced, in the author's system, the FinRL agents and one exploratory reproduction, it lay mainly in fixed allocations, exposure and the evaluation's initial holding rather than in period-by-period decisions (largely from exploratory diagnostics); the ensemble strategy's advantage over equal weight was matched by equal weight under the same turbulence rule (exploratory). These results do not show that reported numbers are wrong or that RL cannot outperform equal weight; the system is one development line and the published methods a selected group.
